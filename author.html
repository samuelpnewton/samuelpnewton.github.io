About

Samuel P. Newton teaches criminal law, criminal procedure, and evidence at the University of Idaho College of Law. A licensed attorney in Colorado, Idaho, Montana, and Utah, he has handled thousands of criminal cases since the early 2000s, including capital defense, complex homicides, and post-conviction appeals that have resulted in appellate reversals. He proudly serves as advisor to OUTLaw, a student LGBTQ+ organization, and has received numerous university awards for teaching, mentorship, research, and diversity, as well as the Distinguished Service Award from the Utah Association of Criminal Defense Lawyers.

An experienced river guide who has navigated major whitewater rapids, Newton spent seven summers leading water sports with the Boy Scouts, and spends his free time telemark skiing, biking, backpacking, and exploring. His legal scholarship focuses on civil disobedience and legal history. Above all, he is a proud father who has also welcomed dozens of foster youth and others into his home over the years.

Selected Cases

  • State v. Wilder, 2018 UT 17, 420 P.3d 1064
    Overruled common-law merger tests for kidnapping convictions, establishing that statutory standards governing separate criminal offenses arising from a single episode control the merger analysis.
  • State v. Ogden, 2018 UT 8, 416 P.3d 1132
    Held as a matter of first impression that a trial court calculating court-ordered complete restitution must specifically determine which pecuniary losses were proximately caused by the defendant’s criminal conduct.
  • State v. Clopten, 2015 UT 82, 362 P.3d 1216
    Addressed evidentiary limits on invoking Fifth Amendment privileges before a jury, standards for penal-interest hearsay exceptions, and constitutional requirements concerning eyewitness identification evidence in a capital murder context.
  • State v. Perea, 2013 UT 68, 322 P.3d 624
    Established that defense expert witness testimony regarding the psychological dynamics of false confessions is admissible in aggravated murder prosecutions.
  • State v. McCullar, 2014 UT App 215, 335 P.3d 900
    Reversed a murder conviction on appeal, holding that the trial court committed reversible, non-harmless error by excluding critical defense testimony regarding statements made by the victim shortly before death.
  • Salt Lake City v. Newman, 2006 UT 69, 148 P.3d 931
    Ruled that a trial de novo appeal from a justice court does not automatically constitute a plain, speedy, and adequate legal remedy, permitting extraordinary writ relief under exceptional circumstances.
