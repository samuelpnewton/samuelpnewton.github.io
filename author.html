About me

Samuel P. Newton is an Associate Professor of Law at the University of Idaho College of Law, where he teaches criminal law, criminal procedure, and evidence. Before moving into academia full-time, he spent roughly two decades as a criminal defense attorney in Utah and Montana, including extensive work on capital cases, and his defense practice included securing the exoneration of a client wrongfully convicted of murder.

He holds a JD from Brigham Young University and a PhD in History from the University of Utah (summa cum laude). His scholarship spans criminal law and procedure, peace and resistance studies, civil disobedience, and legal history, including his forthcoming book, Plows Into Swords: Joseph Smith and Mormon Resistance in Antebellum America, 1805–1844, from Oxford University Press.

Newton has been recognized repeatedly by the University of Idaho for teaching, mentorship, research, and professionalism, and serves as the College of Law’s faculty advisor to OUTLaw, its LGBTQ+ student organization. Above all, he loves his students and genuinely cares about them.

Outside the law school, Newton is as likely to be found on a river as behind a desk. A former whitewater guide and lifelong backpacker and skier, he has also built a barn by hand, restored furniture, and can generally be talked into fixing whatever just broke around the house. He plays trombone, piano, and guitar, reads widely across law, history, philosophy, and spirituality, and has spent time learning from indigenous healing traditions. He is the father of three, including through foster care and adoption, plus a much longer list of former students and honorary kids he has taken in over the years.
